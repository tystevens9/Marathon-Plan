Heart Rate

Pace tells you what to aim for. Heart rate tells you whether today's body can actually deliver it — most useful on easy and long runs, where drift shows up early, especially in Miami humidity. Zones below are set against a 190 bpm cap.

Easy / Long Run
144–160 bpm
Zone 2
Conversational, nose-breathing effort. If HR runs 8–10+ bpm above normal for this pace, slow down — don't push through it.
Marathon Pace
160–167 bpm
Zone 3–4
Comfortably strong — the effort you could hold for hours, not the effort you're fighting to hold.
Tempo
167–175 bpm
Zone 4
Hard but controlled — the "10K-race-in-training" feel.
Interval
175–184 bpm*
Zone 5
Hard, breathing labored. *HR lags pace by 60–90 sec, so on short reps like 800s it often won't climb this high until the rep is nearly over — judge these by pace and feel, not the number.
190 bpm is your ceiling, not a target. These zones are computed off that cap. If any easy run has you sitting near 160+ just to hold easy pace, that's a fatigue/heat signal, not a reason to push harder.
Cardiac drift is a warning light. HR creeps up on long runs even at constant pace, faster in heat and humidity — exactly what Miami training and race day will bring.
Check morning resting HR. 5–10 bpm above your normal for a couple mornings in a row usually means accumulated fatigue. Make the easy run genuinely easy that day.
Race day runs hot. Expect HR a bit higher than training at the same pace from adrenaline and conditions — don't let it panic you into slowing down early if legs and breathing feel fine.

Four Phases

Mileage builds, then cuts back, then tapers — the color runs cool to hot to cool again, tracking effort.

Heat & Humidity

Miami in late January is mild for Florida but still humid — the race starts very early, often before sunrise, for exactly this reason. Plan your morning around the official start time.

Pacing

Don't chase 8:35 in mile 1. Run 10–15 sec/mile slower for the first 3–4 miles, settle into 8:30–8:40 through the middle, and only push under 8:30 in the last 10K if it's there.

Fueling

Rehearse race-day fueling on the 16–20 mile long runs in the Peak phase — same gels, same timing (every 40–45 min), same hydration. Nothing new on race morning.

Ground Rules

First 2 weeks: go by feel.You're re-entering training, not starting from zero — but paces will feel harder than they should for a bit. Normal and temporary.
Cutback weeks aren't optional.Weeks 6, 12, and 15 are where the adaptation from prior weeks actually locks in.
Protect the long run first.If school gets brutal some week, the midweek easy runs are what to cut — not the long run or the key workout.
One missed session changes nothing.A pattern of skipped long runs is the only thing that actually threatens the goal.
